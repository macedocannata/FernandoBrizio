Petrichor, the Smell of Rain

Title Petrichor, o Cheiro da Chuva / Petrichor, the Smell of Rain; Event Porto Design Biennale 2023 — Being Water; Venue Casa do Design, Matosinhos; Dates 19.10.2023 – 03.12.2023; Curated by Fernando Brízio; Cover photo Rain Vases, 2019 © Martín Azúa; Category Curating

After a hot, dry period, the first drops of water fall to the earth and give off the smell of rain. This phenomenon was named petrichor (from the Greek pétros, stone + īchor, ethereal fluid or blood of the gods, as understood in Greek mythology) in 1964 by Australian scientists Isabel Joy Bear and Richard G. Thomas. The aroma, which is often associated with the smell of the earth, is caused by the oils released by some plants during arid and hot periods, which have accumulated on the dry earth and clay. When the first droplets of rain fall, the oils deposited on the earth are suspended in the air in microdroplets which, when inhaled, give us the smell of rain. This odour seems to set off and fill our sensoria, i.e. the part of the brain that is thought to be the common core of all sensations. It’s like a call from the earth, which sometimes leads to introspection and recalling very old memories. It’s a scent that reactivates and creates memories, and that comes to us through water: the rainwater that falls and hits the earth; the water that hydrates plants, that transforms sap and organic matter; the droplets suspended in the air filled with little bits of plants.

The description of this chain of events is a simplification of what actually happens, but it introduces the idea of an aqueous fluid interface. We want to show places of communication, exchange, interaction/intra-action between types of water (in its various states) and between water and other agents, and to address the central role of water in the world. We want to emphasise the ubiquity of water, its presence and circulation in everything, everywhere, as a central element connecting all things. Water moulds everything, it is vital and without it, life would not exist. It can take many forms, manifesting them simultaneously. Water circulates within bodies and between animate and inanimate objects. We humans and more than humans, are connected by a common water flow, we share the same water that enters and exudes from our bodies and which, through biology, is transformed into other materials. Water circulating throughout the Earth, between flesh, plant and mineral matter, aqueous bodies that have shared the same water since the first spark of life millions of years ago, specifically from water.

Petrichor — the clouds, the earth and the sense of smell, the pores of the skin, the roots of lemon balm, the leaves of a holm oak tree, the mycelium below the forest floor, flowers, the aquaporins in cell membranes. These are all natural fluid interfaces, which can be added to by many other human-designed ones. Fluid interfaces such as artificial heart valves, diving helmets, plant rafts, ship traffic trackers, underwater vehicles for mapping underground aquifers, greenhouses, artificial flowers for feeding bees and other pollinating insects, systems for reusing water, calculators for cloning mining waters, synthesisers, sea grass caretakers, the use of bio-pigments.

Petrichor’s content was gathered along six lines based on the curatorial text for this edition of the Porto Design Biennale, Being Water: How we flow together and shape each other:

Cannibals Dancing with Astronauts — Particle Accelerators

More than half of our body is water. The percentage varies with age and differs between men and women. The amount of water in the human body reaches a maximum of 70 per cent in the first few years of life and decreases over the years. We are amniotic beings; we come from water, but we ‘dry out’ throughout our lives.

Based on material or immaterial human elaborations, this body of water can take on a variety of forms in different places, it can walk through space in an EVA suit, swim at the bottom of the ocean or, through music, experience other states of consciousness. It’s possible to have another body, to be another being. These and so many other human elaborations are tools that allow us to access another space and time; they protect us, help us gain knowledge, reorganise and build worlds, deal with fears and the unknown.

Reconnect

In this proposal, we reveal practices that consider collective bodies, co-operation and co-existence. Practices of empathy and sharing, of preservation and healing, hydration, nourishment, repair, talking to strangers, practices of caring for others, all of which are essential gestures for our existence.

Geological Flavour

Every type of water has a different flavour based on the mineral composition absorbed deep within the earth. These are geological flavours, brought to the surface by water from the depths of the earth. The greater or lesser abundance of water, its flavour and other characteristics, such as its hardness, when used in agriculture and food preparation, influence flavour, the identity of a territory and the memories linked to it.

Hydrophilic Objects

The amount of water on Earth is thought to have remained stable for many thousands of years. However, population growth that replicates Western consumption patterns, increased pollution, the retention of water outside of natural systems, deforestation and deregulation of the climate and waterflows on earth mean that water scarcity is increasing dramatically. To enable access to and preservation of water, collection, reuse, purification and transport systems have been developed that operate on the various states of water.

Undercurrents: Hidden Flows

Undercurrents is dedicated to working on representations of water. We show the hidden flows of water, multiple sources and states, scarcity, geopolitical tensions, and disputes between countries. We want to contribute to a broader awareness of water movements and water uses by thinking about the water, humanitarian and climate crises and geopolitical disputes we face.

Walking on Thin Ice

In Greek mythology, Prometheus gave humans fire and the possibility of progress, but it seems that this ability is now leading us towards a furnace. The current capacity for design, by pursuing an idea of progress based on the current prevailing model of happiness and economic growth, turns this human skill into an element that unbalances natural and climate systems. We have become geological agents. Individualism, vanity, greed, and an idea of, what is often superfluous comfort, are depleting the Earth. Growing awareness of the negative impact of human action has led to the development of design processes in a wide variety of areas production seeking to reduce impact on the planet. The list of these processes is endless, but insufficient if not accompanied by widespread changes in individual behaviour and resulting political measures. We will continue, it seems, to head towards a hurricane.
